## Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a clinical Sound Therapy session and a Sound Bath?  
A Sound Bath is usually a group experience where you are "bathed" in ambient sound. Sound Therapy at Flowergrid is typically a one to one clinical session where the frequencies and instruments are tailored specifically to your individual physical and emotional needs. This allows for a much deeper and more targeted approach to your personal wellbeing.

Do I need any previous experience with meditation for Sound Therapy to work?  
No. Sound Therapy is a passive treatment, which makes it an ideal choice for those who find traditional meditation difficult. Your only "job" is to lie comfortably and allow the acoustic vibrations to wash over you. There is no right or wrong way to experience it, and many people find they drift into a state of deep restorative sleep.

Which specific instruments are used to create healing frequencies?  
At Flowergrid, we use a range of high quality instruments including Himalayan and crystal singing bowls, gongs, tuning forks, and percussion tools. Each instrument is chosen for its specific frequency and its ability to encourage brainwave entrainment, helping your mind move from a state of high alert to one of deep repair.

How does acoustic vibration help with physical tension and pain?  
Because the human body is mostly water, sound acts as a physical vibration that travels deeply into your tissues. This helps stimulate blood flow and release tension in the muscles and organs that traditional massage might not reach. While it does not replace medical treatment, it is a powerful supporting tool for managing chronic discomfort.

How many Sound Therapy sessions are typically needed to achieve lasting shifts?  
Most people feel a significant reduction in their stress levels after just one session. However, to achieve long term benefits such as improved sleep patterns or greater emotional resilience, we typically recommend a course of three to five sessions. This allows your nervous system to "learn" the way back to a state of balance more effectively.

Are there specific medical conditions where Sound Therapy is not recommended?  
Sound Therapy is very safe for most people, but there are a few exceptions. It may not be suitable for those with sound induced epilepsy, certain heart conditions (especially if you have a pacemaker), or during the first trimester of pregnancy. We always recommend discussing your health history during your discovery call to ensure it is the right path for you.
